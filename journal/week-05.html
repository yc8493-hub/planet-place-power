Florence: Water

The question

How does water flow through Florence, and how do natural watershed boundaries relate to the city's water supply system?

The data

I used Global Watersheds with MERIT-Hydro (2021) and HydroSHEDS (2013) to delineate the catchment around Palazzo Vecchio in Florence (43.769° N, 11.256° E). For water stress, I used WRI's Aqueduct 4.0 Water Risk dataset through ArcGIS Online. It uses a 1979–2019 baseline, with data from April 2023, and is licensed under CC BY 4.0. Water stress represents the ratio of water withdrawals to available renewable supply at the sub-basin scale.

The method

I selected Palazzo Vecchio in Florence (43.769° N, 11.256° E) as my study site. Using Global Watersheds with MERIT-Hydro (2021) at lower precision, I delineated a catchment of approximately 4,500 km². The model snapped my requested point about 260 m to an outlet at 43.767° N, 11.255° E. I repeated the analysis using MERIT-Hydro at higher precision and HydroSHEDS (2013) at lower precision, obtaining catchment areas of 4,500 and 4,600 km², respectively. For water stress, I used WRI Aqueduct 4.0, which calculates baseline water stress at the sub-basin scale. Florence's water stress value was 0.862 (Extremely High), with a mapped polygon area of 6,071 km². Assuming a site area of 0.1 km², the polygon is approximately 60,710 times larger than my site.

What I found

The three delineations produced similar catchment areas (4,500–4,600 km²), despite differences in outlet snapping. I noticed that part of the watershed boundary crosses Florence's urban area, which made me wonder whether artificial infrastructure could transport water across it. Through further research, I identified the Santa Maria a Mantignano water treatment plant, which receives water from the Arno River near Santa Rosa through a pipeline. However, I could not confirm whether this pipeline crosses my watershed boundary. Although I found the approximate locations of the intake and treatment plant, Global Watersheds does not allow me to mark both locations while keeping the delineated boundary visible. Without overlaying these locations and verifying the pipeline's actual route, I cannot determine whether it crosses the boundary.

What this does not show

I am using the delineated watershed boundary to represent how water moves through Florence, but this proxy may fail because the model only represents surface drainage and excludes artificial water infrastructure, such as the pipeline connecting the Santa Rosa intake to the Mantignano treatment plant. Although I identified this water supply system, I could not verify its exact relationship to the watershed boundary. Therefore, the map cannot establish whether water is transferred across the divide.
