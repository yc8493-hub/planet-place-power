Florence: Land cover

The question

How has the pattern and distribution of land cover in Florence changed over time, and what changes in land use might these maps fail to capture?

The data

I used the Sentinel-2 10m Land Use/Land Cover Time Series by Esri and Impact Observatory to compare Florence in 2017 and 2025, with 2023 as a third reference year. The dataset has a resolution of 10 m and is hosted under the Esri Master License Agreement, while the source data is licensed under CC BY 4.0. I also used the Landsat 1980's Land Use/Land Cover dataset (1984–1992), which has a resolution of 30 m and carries the Esri Master License Agreement without a CC BY clause.

The method

I selected Florence's historic center around Palazzo Vecchio as my study site. Using the Land Cover Explorer, I compared 2017 and 2025 with the Swipe tool. I then compared 2023 with 2025 at the same extent and scale to check whether the pattern remained consistent. Finally, I examined the historical Landsat map and compared the former FIAT industrial site in Novoli with its modern classification.

What I found

The three delineations produced similar catchment areas (4,500–4,600 km²), despite differences in outlet snapping. I noticed that part of the watershed boundary crosses Florence's urban area, which made me wonder whether artificial infrastructure could transport water across it. Through further research, I identified the Santa Maria a Mantignano water treatment plant, which receives water from the Arno River near Santa Rosa through a pipeline. However, I could not confirm whether this pipeline crosses my watershed boundary. Although I found the approximate locations of the intake and treatment plant, Global Watersheds does not allow me to mark both locations while keeping the delineated boundary visible. Without overlaying these locations and verifying the pipeline's actual route, I cannot determine whether it crosses the boundary.

What this does not show

I am using the delineated watershed boundary to represent how water moves through Florence, but this proxy may fail because the model only represents surface drainage and excludes artificial water infrastructure, such as the pipeline connecting the Santa Rosa intake to the Mantignano treatment plant. Although I identified this water supply system, I could not verify its exact relationship to the watershed boundary. Therefore, the map cannot establish whether water is transferred across the divide.
