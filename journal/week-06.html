Florence: Land cover

The question

How has the pattern and distribution of land cover in Florence changed over time, and what changes in land use might these maps fail to capture?

The data

I used the Sentinel-2 10m Land Use/Land Cover Time Series by Esri and Impact Observatory to compare Florence in 2017 and 2025, with 2023 as a third reference year. The dataset has a resolution of 10 m and is hosted under the Esri Master License Agreement, while the source data is licensed under CC BY 4.0. I also used the Landsat 1980's Land Use/Land Cover dataset (1984–1992), which has a resolution of 30 m and carries the Esri Master License Agreement without a CC BY clause.

The method

I selected Florence's historic center around Palazzo Vecchio as my study site. Using the Land Cover Explorer, I compared 2017 and 2025 with the Swipe tool. I then compared 2023 with 2025 at the same extent and scale to check whether the pattern remained consistent. Finally, I examined the historical Landsat map and compared the former FIAT industrial site in Novoli with its modern classification.

Interactive map: View the 2017–2025 land cover comparison .

What I found

Between 2017 and 2025, Crops decreased by 3.5%, while Trees and Built Area each increased by 2.%. The most noticeable changes appeared southwest of the historic center, where the distribution of agricultural land and built-up areas changed. The 2023–2025 map showed a similar pattern, with Crops decreasing by 2.0% and Built Area increasing by 0.5%. However, some changes were not visible in the classification. The former FIAT factory site in Novoli was redeveloped into a mixed-use district with university buildings, housing, and public spaces, potentially benefiting students, residents, and users of public facilities. Yet both the historical and modern maps classified it as Built Area, without showing these changes in land use or who benefited from them.

What this does not show

I am using land cover classifications to represent changes in Florence's urban landscape, but this proxy may fail because the classifier assigns only one category to each pixel. Small gardens, street trees, and vegetation within residential areas may be classified as Built Area. The Novoli example also shows that different land uses can share the same category. Therefore, an unchanged classification does not necessarily mean that the land itself has remained unchanged.
