Florence: Valley and Hills

The question

How do the terrain pattern and slope distribution of Florence shape water movement across different scales?

The data

I used Esri’s Terrain service and the Terrain – Slope Degrees layer. At Florence, the finest underlying elevation dataset is Italy_TINITALY_DTM10m, produced by INGV, with a 10 m cell size and survey dates from 2007–2023. Vertical accuracy (LE90) and the vertical datum are not stated in the layer metadata. For soil, I first used SoilGrids by ISRIC. However, several soil properties returned no readable value over central Florence. I therefore used Esri’s World Soils 250m Soil Organic Carbon layer, which republishes SoilGrids predictions at a 250 m cell size. The original SoilGrids data are licensed under CC BY 4.0; the Esri item page lists the Esri Master License Agreement.

The method

I added Esri’s Terrain and Terrain – Slope Degrees layers in ArcGIS Online. Slope was derived from the elevation raster using a nine-cell window: each cell was calculated from its elevation and the eight neighbouring cells. I first used a continuous Stretch rendering and narrowed the displayed slope range to 0–16°. I then used Classify with manual breaks at 1°, 2°, 5°, and 10°, producing five classes: 0–1°, 1–2°, 2–5°, 5–10°, and 10–16°. The underlying slope data did not change; only the symbology changed.

For soil, I tested several SoilGrids properties over central Florence, but no readable value was returned. I therefore used the World Soils 250m Soil Organic Carbon layer in ArcGIS and checked its description, cell size, licence, and information about the companion uncertainty layer.

What I found

Material would generally move from the steeper hills around Florence toward the lower valley and eventually toward the river. Soil organic carbon shows a patchy distribution around central Florence, with several NoData gaps instead of continuous coverage.

What this does not show

The 10 m terrain cells are too large to show many street-scale features that affect runoff in Florence, such as curbs, drains, and small changes in road slope. The layer metadata also does not state LE90 or the vertical datum, so the vertical accuracy and elevation reference are not fully documented. The soil layer is predictive and also contains NoData areas, so it cannot support a precise site-level soil claim.
