Florence: Story Map

The question

Although the Arno River was essential to Florence's early economic development, the city's modern economy is less dependent on the river's physical resources.

My StoryMap examines this change through the relationship between Florence's physical landscape, water system, land cover, and economic activities.

The data

I reviewed all seven maps from Week 02 to Week 08, checking their spatial units, data origins, and republication conditions.

The method

I selected four maps: Terrain (Week 03), Water (Week 05), Land Cover (Week 06), and Industry (Week 07). I arranged them to move from the Arno's physical environment to Florence's modern economy. Terrain introduces the valley where Florence developed, and Water shows the larger river system. Land Cover then moves to the modern urban landscape, while Industry provides a broader view of trade and tourism-related locations. The spatial units also change across the sequence: from 10 m terrain cells to a watershed boundary, then 10 m land cover cells and national country polygons. These maps describe different geographical scales, so their results cannot be directly compared as measurements of the same area.

My published StoryMap brings together these four maps to explore the changing role of the Arno River in Florence's economic development. URL: https://arcg.is/1iuTfS8

What I found

Putting these maps together reveals a distinction between the Arno River's physical influence and Florence's economic dependence on it. The city still occupies the valley shaped by the river, but its modern land cover and wider economic connections do not necessarily reflect a continued dependence on the river for transportation or water power.

What this does not show

However, I lack a historical map showing the locations of mills, textile workshops, and trade routes along the Arno. Without this evidence and more localized economic data, I cannot directly compare Florence's dependence on the river in the past and today.
