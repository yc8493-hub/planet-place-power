Florence: Land cover

The question

How visible is Florence's digital infrastructure, and how does its distribution reflect the city's character?

The data

I used three sources for this week's research, all accessed on October 29, 2026. OpenStreetMap (OSM) is a community-created database available under the Open Database License (ODbL). Maps based on its data can be published with attribution and the required licence notice. TeleGeography's Submarine Cable Map allows screenshots to be republished under CC BY-SA 4.0 with credit, but its underlying data cannot be freely redistributed. Data Center Map, published by Data Center Map ApS, does not allow its content to be copied or republished without permission. Therefore, I only used it as a reference.

The method

I used Overpass Turbo to search for telecom and power infrastructure within the same area around Florence. The telecom query searched for all objects tagged telecom, while the power query searched for power=substation and power=tower. In both queries, the predicate determined what infrastructure to search for, and {{bbox}} limited the search to the visible map extent. I ran both queries through the default Overpass API server (https://overpass-api.de/api/) on October 29, 2026, then exported the power data as GeoJSON and imported it into ArcGIS Online.

I did not add a second layer for this week's because the telecom query returned no results, while Data Center Map does not allow its data to be copied or republished without permission.

What I found

I found that Florence's physical geography is connected to its economic development. The Arno River historically supported transportation and trade, and it is also part of the city's landscape today. This connection holds when considering Florence's tourism industry, especially how its historical environment attracts visitors. However, the same connection does not fully explain tourism across Italy. Travel & Tourism was the largest export category shown in the Atlas, but the activities are distributed across different places. I chose the Uffizi Gallery in Florence, the Colosseum in Rome, and Fiumicino Airport as three representative points. Fiumicino is Italy's busiest airport, which also shows why I should not limit the study to Florence when analysing.

What this does not show

I am using mapped power infrastructure to stand for the electricity network around Florence, and it might fail because OpenStreetMap relies on what contributors can identify and record. Electricity towers are relatively easy to recognize, while underground cables and facilities inside buildings may not be recorded. Therefore, zero telecom results in Overpass map do not mean that Florence has no telecommunications infrastructure. They only show what is missing from this particular dataset.

AI use: I consulted ChatGPT when I was unsure how to deal with the absence of submarine cable landing points and telecom data in Florence and decided not to add a second layer and kept my original map.
